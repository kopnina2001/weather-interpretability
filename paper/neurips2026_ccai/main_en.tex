\title{Interpretability Methods for Transformer-Based Weather Forecasting Models}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We study how an error in one input field propagates through a multivariate weather forecast. In Aurora and Pangu-Weather, one of 19 fields is replaced by climatology while the other inputs stay fixed. Forty-eight dates yield directed response matrices at +6 and +24 h, dose curves, and spatial maps. In both models, replacing $Z850$ changes $V850$ about nine times more than the reverse intervention. The direct trace of a $Z500$ perturbation shrinks by +24 h, although errors remain in other fields. Lower-level geopotential has the broadest effects on temperature, humidity, and wind; the possible contribution of terrain has not yet been separated from vertical coupling. The audit ranks error-transfer paths and defines testable field- and latitude-weighting schemes for training. It describes how a model uses information, not causal relations in the atmosphere.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Pangu-Weather and Aurora produce global, high-resolution forecasts at low marginal cost~\citep{bi2023pangu,bodnar2025aurora}. RMSE and ACC measure final skill but do not identify which input field produced an error in another output or whether a damaged channel can be reconstructed from neighboring variables. This matters for climate services because equal mean skill can hide different sensitivity to analysis and observation errors~\citep{wmo2025earlywarning}.

Weather-model interpretation uses gradients, perturbations, and representation analysis~\citep{yang2024survey}. Recent studies identified advection heads in Aurora~\citep{macmillan2026advection}, examined latent directions in GraphCast~\citep{tempest2026tool}, and imposed controlled chemical perturbations on Aurora~\citep{hu2026chemistry}. Our observable is the directed response of every physical output rather than an internal network component, so the same audit can be used for a model whose activations are unavailable.

We replace one field by seasonal-diurnal climatology and measure the changes in all 19 outputs. A full matrix identifies errors that spread widely, six replacement levels quantify response size, and maps locate the added error. The study concerns diagnostics for models used in climate services and adaptation. It does not estimate climate attribution or emission reductions.

\section{Methods}

ERA5 provides initial states and verification truth~\citep{hersbach2020era5}; WeatherBench 2 climatology is matched by day of year and UTC hour and is computed over 1990--2019~\citep{rasp2024weatherbench2}. We use Aurora (1.3B parameters; two input times) and Pangu-Weather (256M; one input time) on a $0.25^\circ$ grid. The main sample has 48 initializations in 2024: four dates per month, balanced over 00/06/12/18 UTC. The fields are MSLP, 10-m $u/v$, 2-m temperature, and $Z,Q,T,u,v$ at 1000, 850, and 500 hPa. Both Aurora input times are patched. A separate seven-field Aurora check spans 2024--2025 (Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}).

Input field $v$ is replaced by climatology. For output $u$, lead $\tau$, and date set $\mathcal D$, we report
\begin{align}
S_{vu}(\tau)&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal D|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal D}
\frac{\rmse_w(\hat y_u^{(v)},\hat y_u^{(0)})}{\sigma_w(y_u)}, \\
\Delta\acc_{vu}(\tau)&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal D|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal D}
\left[\acc_w(\hat y_u^{(v)},y_u,c_u)-\acc_w(\hat y_u^{(0)},y_u,c_u)\right].
\end{align}
Spatial sums use normalized $\cos\phi$ weights. $S$ measures distance from the baseline forecast; negative $\Delta\acc$ records a loss of anomaly skill against ERA5. For the dose sweep, $Z1000$ is mixed with climatology at $\alpha\in\{0,.2,.4,.6,.8,1\}$; spatial composites compute temporal RMSE at each grid cell. The patch retains season and UTC hour but creates an inconsistent multivariate state. Thus $\alpha=1$ is a finite stress test, not a local gradient or evidence of atmospheric causality.

\subsection{Direction and attenuation}

The matrices are asymmetric. At +6 h, $S_{Z850,V850}$ is $0.38$ for Aurora and $0.47$ for Pangu, while the reverse entries are $0.04$ and $0.05$. The roughly ninefold contrast recurs in both architectures. Diagonal $S_{Z500,Z500}$ falls from $0.08$ to $0.04$ in Aurora and from $0.19$ to $0.09$ in Pangu between +6 and +24 h. This is a return toward the baseline trajectory, not demonstrated improvement against ERA5: off-diagonal errors remain.

\subsection{Dose and spatial footprint}

All six $Z1000$ doses rise without a plateau. The $T1000$ response slope in Aurora falls from $0.690$ to $0.217$ between +6 and +24 h, while Pangu rises from $0.406$ to $0.614$. The models do not damp every perturbation. After full replacement, mean per-cell $T1000$ RMSE increases by $6.02/2.01\times$ in Aurora and $3.35/3.06\times$ in Pangu at +6/+24 h. The increase covers 99--100\% of cells, with different latitude profiles across models.

Diagonal attenuation and transfer to neighboring fields are parts of the same rollout. The model reconstructs part of $Z500$ from context after the inconsistency has already changed other outputs. This should not be called forecast improvement until the additional error against ERA5 is measured at several leads. The matrix is also not a correlation matrix: each direction requires a separate run.

Lower-tropospheric geopotential gives the broadest response. $Z1000$ and $Z850$ retain sharper effects of terrain and below-ground extrapolation than $Z500$. The experiment does not separate this from vertical coupling; it needs a below-ground mask and terrain-height strata. A second corruption is also needed because full climatology replacement leaves the distribution of training states.

The matrix ranks outward field influence, the dose slope supplies a finite response coefficient, and latitude profiles locate the added error. These quantities can select $\lambda_N$ and field-specific $w_N(\phi)$ in $\mathcal L=\sum_N\lambda_N\sum_s w_N(\phi_s)\ell_N(s)$. This is a matched retraining plan, not a demonstrated gain.

A smaller seven-field Aurora audit provides a temporal check on 48 dates spanning 2024--2025. It retains a low $Z500$ diagonal and the stronger $Z850\!\to V850$ direction, but its different field set and lack of Pangu forecasts prevent pooling it with the main matrix.

The main limitations are 48 dates in 2024, two leads, no date-block intervals, and source arrays that were unavailable during paper assembly. The month-block bootstrap added to the repository must be run before submission. Further checks should measure additional error against ERA5, mask below-ground points, and compare the climatology patch with an anomaly from another date matched by season and UTC. The appendix specifies ensemble audits for GenCast, FuXi-S2S, and LaDCast and retrospective ERA5 replacement within a rollout. For climate services, this remains a failure-mode audit: it exposes brittle channels but does not itself improve skill or validate a user's decision.

\section{Conclusion}

Paired replacement reveals directed transfer hidden by aggregate scores. Aurora and Pangu attenuate the direct trace of some perturbations while leaving errors in other variables; both reproduce the strong $Z850\!\to V850$ direction. Lower-level geopotential has the broadest response, although the role of terrain still needs a control. Matrices, dose curves, and maps form a reproducible channel audit and nominate retraining experiments. Claims of improved skill require uncertainty intervals, orographic controls, and matched training runs.

\subsection{Data splits and matrix conventions}
\label{app:provenance}

The repository contains several evaluation sets. Its ERA5 cache and broad forecast-verification campaign cover 2024--2025. The paired results in the main paper use a narrower common set: 48 dates from 2024 for both models, all 19 fields, and leads of +6 and +24 h. A separate Aurora-only audit uses seven fields and 48 dates split across 2024--2025. We report that audit below as a temporal check and do not pool it with the two-model estimates.

Every matrix uses rows for patched inputs and columns for affected outputs. $S_{vu}\geq0$ measures displacement from the unpatched forecast after normalization by the verified field scale. A large diagonal entry means that the model cannot reconstruct that input from the other 18 fields; an off-diagonal entry records propagation into another output. $\Delta\acc_{vu}<0$ means that the same intervention reduces anomaly skill against ERA5. Color limits differ by model, lead, and metric. Cross-panel comparisons therefore use the cell values, not hue.

\subsection{Statistical checks before submission}
\label{app:statistics}

The resampling unit is the initialization date, not a matrix cell. Cells share input states and are not independent observations. The added \texttt{analysis/bootstrap\_19var.py} script groups the four dates in each calendar month, resamples the 12 month blocks with replacement, and applies the same sample to Aurora and Pangu. Each of 5000 replicates recomputes the mean matrices. The outputs are 95\% intervals for every cell, the difference $S_{Z850,V850}-S_{V850,Z850}$, the diagonal ratio $S_{Z500,Z500}(24)/S_{Z500,Z500}(6)$, and the area-weighted cross-model correlation.

The current figures contain point estimates. An earlier comparison script reported $r=0.913$ from unweighted \texttt{rel\_sens}, whereas the paper displays \texttt{rel\_sens\_w}. The script now defaults to area-weighted metrics, and the cross-model correlation is withheld until the source tables are rerun. The generated \texttt{weighted\_matrix\_ci.csv} and \texttt{key\_results\_bootstrap.csv} should be the sole numerical source for the final manuscript.

Attenuation in $S$ measures return toward the baseline trajectory. A claim of correction against truth additionally requires
\begin{equation}
D_u^{(v)}(\tau)=\rmse_w(\hat y_u^{(v)}(\tau),y_u(\tau))-\rmse_w(\hat y_u^{(0)}(\tau),y_u(\tau)).
\end{equation}
A decline in $D_v^{(v)}$ across leads together with positive $D_u^{(v)}$ for $u\ne v$ would demonstrate recovery of the patched field and transfer to its neighbors. This test should use at least three leads; the stored +6 and +24 h results provide only two points.

Two controls separate model structure from patch construction. For lower pressure levels, grid cells where surface pressure is below 1000 or 850 hPa should be excluded, with lowlands and high terrain reported separately. To test distribution shift, the climatology patch should be replaced by an anomaly from another date matched by season and UTC, optionally scaled to the same perturbation norm. The main pattern is stable only if the asymmetry sign, leading inputs, and matrix ranks persist.

\subsection{Candidate field and latitude weights}
\label{app:training}

Each input yields three summaries: retained diagonal response $R_M$, total off-diagonal influence $I_M$, and latitude profile $E_{MN}(\phi)$. The first separates fields that the model partly reconstructs; the second identifies sources of broad transfer; the third shows where a common $\cos\phi$ weight misses output-specific error structure. These quantities must be normalized and fixed before training. New weights require a matched comparison with identical data, optimization budget, and initialization seeds.

\subsection{Ensemble audit and mid-rollout field correction}
\label{app:future}

The paired audit should be extended to probabilistic systems with shared random seeds between baseline and patched members. GenCast samples diffusion-based 15-day ensembles~\citep{price2025gencast}; FuXi-S2S generates 42-day ensembles through learned and initial-condition perturbations~\citep{chen2024fuxis2s}; LaDCast generates hourly ensemble forecasts with a transformer-based diffusion model operating in a learned latent space~\citep{zhuang2025ladcast}. An ensemble-mean comparison is insufficient; the report should include distributions of $S$ and $\Delta\acc$, change in CRPS, and spread--skill behavior.

A second experiment should replace one predicted field with verifying ERA5 at +6, +12, +24, or +48 h and continue the rollout. Reduced downstream error would locate a correctable transfer pathway; renewed error in the replaced field would measure recurrence. This ground-truth intervention is a retrospective oracle test, not an operational forecast procedure. Running the same replacement for each field and lead would separate errors that the model damps, errors that recur, and errors that contaminate neighboring fields.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$Z1000$}{Z1000} dose response}

The dose panels show mean normalized error at six measured values of $\alpha$ and a least-squares slope through those points. The intercept includes baseline forecast error, and the slope describes the finite path to climatology. Without sweeps over other inputs, it is not a general law that $X\%$ noise in any field produces $Y\%$ error in another. The opposing lead-time changes in Aurora and Pangu instead show why the coefficient must remain model-specific.

\subsection{Verified skill rows and spatial composites}

The $\Delta\acc$ rows test whether forecast displacement also reduces anomaly skill. Spatial composites show where added temporal RMSE occurs; they are not signed bias maps. The fractions of degraded cells and RMSE ratios below are unweighted grid-cell summaries and must be distinguished from the area-weighted global metrics used in the matrices.
